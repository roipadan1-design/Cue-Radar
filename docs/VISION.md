# Cue Radar — Product Vision

Written 2026-09-16, by the research pass documented in `docs/RESEARCH_market_scan.md`. This file describes what Cue Radar is *for* and where each of its three pillars is going, grounded in the competitive gap analysis and in the architecture already committed in `docs/HANDOFF_V3.md` and `docs/ROADMAP.md`. Where this document proposes something not yet in HANDOFF_V3 or the roadmap, it says so explicitly — nothing here overrides the existing data model or task ledger; it explains the destination those tasks are heading toward.

**One line:** Cue Radar is a Career OS for independent contemporary dance, performance and experimental-sound artists working across ~23 European/Mediterranean/East-Asian scenes — a place that tells you what's open, what's on where you're headed, and gives you one page that shows who you are.

**Why now (from the gap analysis):** no existing product combines a performing-arts-native opportunities feed, an explicit verification/trust layer, a location-aware "what to do while I'm here" surface, and a career-identity profile that has a reason to be opened weekly rather than sitting dormant like a resume. Each piece exists somewhere in isolation (see `docs/RESEARCH_market_scan.md` Part 3); nobody has bundled them for this audience.

## Pillar 1 — Hub

**What it is today:** the opportunities feed. Already the most built-out pillar (Phases P1–P2 in `docs/ROADMAP.md`; Tasks 01–08). Server-rendered list of `hub_feed` rows — open calls, residencies, grants, co-productions, commissions, festival submissions, awards, lab/workshops, auditions/jobs, mentorships — sorted by deadline, grouped `Closing this week / This month / Later / Rolling`, filterable by market/type/discipline, with per-row Fit signals, financial toggles (no-fee, funded, covers), trust stamps (verified date, last-checked date, source), save-to-pipeline, and ICS export.

**What makes it different from the alternatives found in research:**
- **Performing-arts-native, not visual-art-with-a-dance-tag.** Res Artis, TransArtists, ArtConnect, CaFÉ and e-flux all default to a visual-arts taxonomy (exhibitions, galleries, juried shows) with dance/performance/sound as one filter among many. Cue Radar's controlled vocabulary (`docs/HANDOFF_V3.md` Part D) starts from dance/choreography/performance/sound/live_electronics/installation/interdisciplinary as the primary axis.
- **A feed, not a database.** TransArtists and Res Artis are point-in-time search tools you visit when you remember to. The Hub's deadline-grouped, sorted-by-urgency structure (and the eventual weekly digest, Task 18) is designed to be a habit, the way a music discovery app is, not a reference desk you consult twice a year.
- **Verification as a stated feature, not an assumption.** No competitor reviewed advertises "verified by a person, on this date, re-checked after 30 days" as a visible product feature — most rely on the listing organization self-posting and self-maintaining. Trust stamps (Task 08) make Cue Radar's curation legible to the artist, which matters directly to the "never fabricate" ethic this whole project operates under.
- **Continuous, cross-institution.** The dance-specific alternatives that do exist (Aerowaves, Life Long Burning, dancEUA — see market scan Part 1) are each tied to a single funding cycle or partner list and publish a handful of calls a year. The Hub is designed to aggregate across many sources continuously via the sheet → sync pipeline.

**Near-term work still needed (already scoped, not repeated here):** Supabase wiring (Task 04), seed/sync alignment to reach ≥60 live opportunities (Task 05), calendar/ICS (Task 06), Fit score and financial toggles (Task 07), trust stamps and OG images (Task 08). The vision does not change any of this — it is the foundation everything else stands on.

**Where the Hub should go beyond what's already scoped, informed by the gap analysis:**
- **Discipline-specific structured fields the visual-art competitors don't have and don't need**, e.g. for performing-arts calls: technical rider requirements, ensemble/company-size eligibility, touring-format expectations (single work vs. repertoire). This is a genuine differentiator versus ArtConnect/Res Artis/CaFÉ, none of which model performance-specific submission requirements. Not urgent for pilot, but should be on the radar as the schema matures — flag as a future `opportunities` migration, not a v1 requirement.
- **A "why this matched you" line** tied to the Fit score (Task 07) — the one thing that makes the Hub feel personal rather than a static list, and something none of the directory-style competitors (TransArtists, Res Artis) attempt because they have no artist profile to match against.

## Pillar 2 — Trip Radar

**What it is:** when an artist's profile shows they'll be in a given city for a coming period (current_city / current_city_from / current_city_until, or a future `trips` table per Task 11), Cue Radar proactively surfaces what's worth their time there: performances, workshops, festivals, retreats, studio drop-ins — everything an artist traveling or relocating short-term looks for but has no single place to find, especially across a discipline boundary (a dance artist wants to know about the experimental-sound night too, and vice versa).

**Why this is real white space, not assumed:** the market scan's closest analog — Bandsintown/Songkick — solves a structurally different problem: promoting a *musician's own booked tour dates* to *that musician's existing fans*. Nothing in that category (or anywhere else checked) helps an artist arriving in a new city discover *other people's* programming across disciplines. On the Move covers cross-border *funding* for mobility but not "what's on while I'm there." This gap held up under direct research, not assumption.

**Concrete shape (per `docs/HANDOFF_V3.md` Part E, `app/radar/[city]/page.tsx`):**
- v1 (Task 11, already scoped): deadlines closing in a `?from=&to=` window for that city, plus public profiles of other artists whose `current_city` overlaps the traveler's dates.
- Task 12 (already scoped, sourced by `docs/RESEARCH_trip_radar_sources.md`): manually curated `event_sources` for 6 pilot cities (Berlin, Cologne, Brussels, Tel Aviv, Vienna, Amsterdam), ~15 sources each, feeding an `events` table and a per-trip brief.
- The 23-city event-source research already done (`docs/RESEARCH_trip_radar_sources.md`) found **no public ICS/RSS/JSON feed for any source across all 23 markets** — the binding constraint is that Trip Radar's data pipeline must be built around HTML/DOM scraping and JSON-LD (`schema.org/Event`) extraction, escalating to headless-browser rendering for JS-rendered calendars, not a feed reader. This is an engineering fact, not a vision detail, but it directly shapes how ambitious Trip Radar's coverage can be in the near term — expect uneven, human-curated coverage before automated (Task 13–14 agent) coverage is trustworthy enough to expand it.

**A plausible future revenue path — flagged, not designed:** once Trip Radar reliably surfaces venues, studios, and businesses an artist is likely to visit while in a city (rehearsal spaces, late-night venues, artist-friendly cafés/bars, gear rental, workshop hosts), there is a plausible partnership / revenue-share model with those venues and businesses — e.g., a featured-listing fee, a booking referral fee, or a paid placement above the organic ranking. **This is explicitly a future direction only.** It should not shape data-model or UX decisions now: Trip Radar's v1 must stay curation-first and free of pay-to-rank distortion so artists trust it the way they're meant to trust the Hub's verification stamps. Revisit only after Trip Radar has real usage data showing which categories of surfaced venues/businesses artists actually act on.

## Pillar 3 — Artist profile

**Owner's framing:** "a LinkedIn for artists" — one clean page per artist (photo, bio, where they are now and where they'll be in the coming months, links to socials, photos, showreel/video, who inspires them) plus LinkedIn-style connections and follows. The owner has flagged this as needing further development — it is, deliberately, the least-defined pillar here, and the one this research is best positioned to sharpen because the closest comparable products have real, checkable track records.

**What the research says, concretely:**

1. **LinkedIn itself is the wrong shape to copy, not just a competitor to differentiate from.** LinkedIn's entire data model — job title, employer, "experience" as a list of positions — assumes a career made of jobs at companies. An independent artist's career is a rotating set of gigs, residencies, self-produced work, grants and company memberships across cities; forcing that into LinkedIn's schema is precisely the friction artists currently route around by using an Instagram grid or a Squarespace site instead. Cue Radar's profile fields in `docs/HANDOFF_V3.md` Part D (`current_city`, `current_city_from/until`, `disciplines`, `showreel_url`, `open_for_collab`, `available_from`) are already closer to the right shape — they describe a practice in motion, not a resume. The vision should lean further into this rather than importing LinkedIn conventions like "endorsements" or "recommendations," which don't map to how artists actually vouch for each other.

2. **Polywork is the cautionary tale, and it is directly instructive, not just a data point.** Polywork raised $40M+ specifically to build a "portfolio, not job titles" identity network for exactly this psychographic — creatives who found LinkedIn's format alienating — and shut down January 31, 2025 after a 2023 pivot into standalone personal-website-building failed to create a reason to return. The clear lesson: **a profile-only product, however well-designed, does not survive on its own** — it needs a reason to be opened weekly that isn't the profile itself. Cue Radar already has that reason built into the other two pillars (the Hub is a feed you check for deadlines; Trip Radar is checked before and during travel). The profile pillar's job is to be the *byproduct* of using those — filled in gradually because you're already in the app — not a destination you visit to update a bio. Concretely: profile completeness should be nudged from Hub/Trip Radar moments ("add your current city to see what's on there" rather than a standalone "complete your profile" campaign).

3. **Portfolio-site builders (Cargo, Format, Adobe Portfolio/Behance) solve presentation but not discovery or connection — and that split is useful.** Cargo in particular has real standing in the contemporary-art/design world for minimal, gallery-like visual presentation. Cue Radar's public profile page (`app/a/[handle]`) should hold its own on presentation quality — max-width layout, large showreel, quiet typography, per Task 02's "museum wall label" register — but its actual differentiation is that it is *networked*: discoverable through the Hub (who applied/saved this opportunity, with consent), through Trip Radar (who else is in this city right now), and eventually through follows/connections (Task 17). A beautiful standalone site (what Cargo/Format sell) doesn't get found; a profile embedded in a feed people already check does.

4. **Concrete directions for "needs further development," grounded in what's missing elsewhere:**
   - **"Where I am now / where I'll be" as the profile's organizing fact, not a line item.** No competitor reviewed — not LinkedIn, not ArtConnect, not any portfolio builder — treats an artist's current and upcoming location as a first-class, structured, date-ranged field the way Cue Radar's schema already does. This is the profile's actual hook into Trip Radar and should be visually the most prominent structured fact on the public page, ahead of a job-title-shaped headline.
   - **"Who inspires them" as a light, human field, kept simple.** The owner named it explicitly; nothing in the competitive set does anything like it (LinkedIn has no equivalent; Are.na's channels are adjacent in *spirit* — curated references — but are a general research tool, not a profile field). Keep it to a short freeform or lightly-structured list (names/links), not a taxonomy — it's a texture detail, not infrastructure, and over-engineering it risks the "generic/AI-generated" register Task 02 explicitly warns against.
   - **Discipline and company/ensemble context that LinkedIn's schema has no field for**: which company/ensemble (if any), solo vs. collaborative practice, current open-for-collab status — already partly modeled (`open_for_collab`, `disciplines`) and should stay central to how a profile reads, since it's the information a presenter or festival curator (the realistic "who looks at this page" audience, alongside peer artists) actually needs.
   - **Presentation quality as a competitive requirement, not a nice-to-have**, given Cargo/Format's real reputation in this exact adjacent aesthetic space — showreel treatment, image handling and typography on `app/a/[handle]` need to hold up next to a dedicated portfolio site, or artists will keep maintaining both and treat Cue Radar's profile as secondary.

5. **What "LinkedIn-style connections and follows" should mean here, kept intentionally light for now:** a follow (one-directional, low-commitment) and a connection (mutual, higher-commitment) between artist profiles, surfaced from natural touchpoints — same city/dates on Trip Radar, same discipline tag, same opportunity saved (with consent) — rather than a generic "people you may know" import. This is a v1-appropriate description of Task 17's `follows`/`connections` tables and `/inbox`; it does not require new product design beyond what's already in the roadmap.

**Explicitly parked — do not design yet:** the artist-connection/matchmaking layer (proactively recommending specific collaborators by location, date and discipline overlap) is intentionally out of scope for this document and for near-term roadmap work. It deserves dedicated research of its own — on consent, on what "good match" even means for artists (a shared discipline and city overlap is necessary but nowhere near sufficient), and on moderation/trust implications once the product actively introduces strangers to each other rather than just letting them find each other. Mentioned here only so it isn't silently forgotten; nothing above should be read as a first draft of that feature.

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Trust is the product, not a feature.** Every pillar above leans on the same thing the market scan found nobody else makes explicit: verified data (Hub), curated-not-algorithmic surfacing (Trip Radar), and an identity layer that doesn't ask artists to perform a career they don't have (Profile). This is the throughline, and it is also why `docs/HANDOFF_V3.md` Part H's "never fabricate" rule and this research role's own operating rule are the same rule applied twice.
- **Sequencing matches the existing roadmap.** This document does not propose reordering P1–P7 in `docs/ROADMAP.md`. Hub is furthest along because it's the easiest pillar to verify and the one with the clearest existing data pipeline (the sheet). Trip Radar is gated on manual source curation before automation (Tasks 11–14) because the 23-city research found no shortcut (no public feeds anywhere). Profile's "further development" is primarily a sequencing and content question, not a missing-table question — most of the schema for the ideas above already exists in `docs/HANDOFF_V3.md` Part D.
