# Fellow. — Competitive Landscape and Gap Analysis

Written 2026-09-16. Scope: who else operates in (a) opportunity/open-call aggregation for artists and (b) "career profile for creatives" products, and where each falls short for independent contemporary dance, performance and experimental-sound artists working across cities and disciplines — the audience Fellow. serves.

**Method note:** every product below was checked via live web search on 2026-09-16 (search results and, where fetched, page content). Every claim carries its source. Nothing here is inferred from a product's name or category — if a fact (pricing, scale, feature) could not be confirmed from a source, it is marked **unverified** or omitted. This file replaces the previous contents of `docs/RESEARCH_market_scan.md`, which were actually a Trip Radar event-source mapping unrelated to this competitive scan; that content has been preserved unchanged at `docs/RESEARCH_trip_radar_sources.md` and is still the source Task 12 should use.

## Part 1 — Opportunity / open-call aggregators

### General visual-arts-first directories (broad reach, not dance/sound-specific)

| Product | What it actually does | Who it serves | Price (public) | Where it falls short for THIS audience |
|---|---|---|---|---|

In 2024, artists were the most common target group for its calls (64.8%) and 18.8% of calls were open to emerging artists. No structured discipline or deadline filtering — it's a chronological announcement feed, not a database. No profile or matching. |

Paid **Opportunities Plan**: $4/mo billed yearly ($48/yr) or $5/mo monthly. Paid **Artist Plan** (adds more): $12/mo billed yearly or $15/mo monthly. [Search-result pricing summary](https://www.artconnect.com/artists) | Closest single competitor in *shape* (feed + profile + save), but visual-arts-first taxonomy, no performing-arts fields (touring dates, company/ensemble size, technical requirements), no city-based "what's on while I'm here" layer, and it paywalls the deadline-alert/opportunity-tracking feature that Fellow. plans to give free to signed-in users. |

[How it Works](https://www.callforentry.org/how-it-works/) | Backend/infrastructure product, not a discovery feed for artists. No unified cross-organization feed with real filtering by discipline/city/deadline in the way Fellow.'s Hub is designed. |

|---|---|---|---|---|

| **DanceAuditionss** ([danceauditionss.com/open-calls](https://www.danceauditionss.com/open-calls/)) | Curated listing site for dance/performance open calls and auditions (e.g., Tilburg in Motion Dance Festival). Appears to be a small independent curator, not an institutional network — **scale and update frequency unverified**; no "about" page content was retrieved confirming who runs it. | Dancers/performers looking for auditions and festival calls. | Not confirmed (site appears free to browse). | Small, apparently manually curated, unverifiable scale/reliability — flagged as **needs direct verification** before treating as a serious comparator. |

**Observation on the dance/performance segment specifically:** unlike visual art, which has several large cross-institutional aggregators (Res Artis, TransArtists, e-flux, ArtConnect), contemporary dance/performance discovery is fragmented across small, often EU-project-funded, single-mandate networks (Aerowaves = one annual cohort call; Life Long Burning and dancEUA = project-bound, partner-tied calls). None of them function as a continuously updated, filterable, cross-institution feed the way Fellow.'s Hub is designed to. Experimental sound is even less served — no dedicated aggregator for it surfaced in this research; the closest adjacent infrastructure is general mobility/touring information (Resident Advisor for the electronic-music gig calendar side, covered under Trip Radar comparators below, not opportunities).

## Part 2 — "Career profile for creatives" products

| Product | What it actually does | Who it serves | Price | Where it falls short for THIS audience |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | The reference point: professional profile + connections/follows + feed + jobs. Universally known; no citation needed for basic function. | Everyone, generalist. | Free tier + paid Premium tiers (not itemized here — out of scope, well known). | Built for corporate/knowledge-work careers: job titles, companies, "experience" as employment history. No native fields for touring dates, current city / upcoming city, showreel/video-first presentation, discipline taxonomy (choreography vs. sound design vs. dramaturgy), or "who inspires you." Artists who use it do so by bending a resume-shaped tool to a practice-shaped career — it works but nothing about it is built for a person whose "job" is a rotating set of gigs, residencies and self-produced work across cities. |
| **Polywork** | Was a professional-identity platform explicitly positioned against LinkedIn's job-title model — a personalized portfolio page highlighting projects/collaborations/side-work rather than employers, with website-builder/hosting/CMS features. Founded 2018, raised $40M+ (investors included Stripe's Collison brothers). **Shut down January 31, 2025** — growth stalled, a 2023 pivot to personal-website-building didn't stick, subdomains no longer active. [Substack retrospective](https://promptledproduct.substack.com/p/what-happened-to-polywork-an-eye), [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42554740) | (Formerly) knowledge workers and creatives wanting a less resume-shaped profile. | N/A — defunct. | Directly relevant as a **cautionary data point**, not a live competitor: a well-funded, well-positioned "portfolio-first, not job-title-first" identity product for exactly the psychographic Fellow.'s artist profile targets still failed to escape LinkedIn's network effect. The lesson for Fellow.'s profile pillar: a standalone social-identity product needs a reason to open the app beyond the profile itself (LinkedIn has jobs/feed/recruiting; Polywork had none of that pull). Fellow.'s Hub and Trip Radar are exactly that kind of pull if the profile pillar is built as a byproduct of using them, not a destination on its own. |
| **Contra** ([contra.com/pricing](https://contra.com/pricing)) | Portfolio-first freelance marketplace (launched 2020) connecting freelancers and clients; positions itself as commission-free for freelancers (client pays the platform fee instead — a scaled fee from $2 up to $29 per project plus payment processing). Free plan (profile, proposals, payments, no commission); Pro $29/mo; Max $79/mo. Work categories dominated by design/product/marketing/web development. | Freelance knowledge/creative workers seeking paid client work — a marketplace, not a career-identity network. | Free / $29 / $79 per month. [Pricing](https://contra.com/pricing) | Transactional marketplace for hire-a-freelancer work, not a career/identity layer for artists whose income comes from grants, fees, residencies and box office rather than client projects. | **Are.na** ([are.na/about](https://www.are.na/about), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Are.na)) | Creative-research/curation network (launched 2015) for collecting and connecting images, links, text and files into shared "channels" — closer to a collaborative mood-board/research tool than a career profile. No ads, no likes, artist-founded (Charles Broskoski + collaborators), shifted toward user ownership via a Republic crowdraise ($145,568 raised). | Designers, artists, researchers who want a quiet, non-algorithmic space to think in public. | Free to browse; premium plan for unlimited "blocks," with 50% student discount. | Not a career-profile or opportunities product at all — no bio/CV fields, no deadline tracking, no "where I am now." Relevant to Fellow. only as a *design/tone reference* (the Task 02 register cites Are.na explicitly), not a functional competitor. |
| **Portfolio-site builders** — Adobe Portfolio, Behance, Cargo, Format | **Adobe Portfolio**: free with any Creative Cloud plan (Creative Cloud starts at $11.99/mo) or via Behance Pro ($9.99/mo annual); simple personal site builder, no ecommerce, limited customization. **Behance**: Adobe-owned (since Dec 2012) creative-showcase network, free to post; Behance Pro ($9.99/mo annual) removes sales commission and adds analytics + Portfolio access. **Cargo**: ~$13/mo, has a real following in contemporary-art/design for its minimal, gallery-like visual presentation, not built for blogging/SEO/selling. **Format**: photography-workflow-first (client proofing, file delivery). An artist using one still needs somewhere separate to find opportunities and be found by peers/presenters. They compete for the "profile" pillar's *presentation* layer but not its *networking* or *discovery* layer. |

## Part 3 — Gap analysis: the white space

Cross-referencing what was actually found (not assumed) against Fellow.'s three pillars:

1. **No product combines a cross-discipline, cross-type opportunities feed (open calls + residencies + grants + festivals + submissions) with performing-arts-specific structure (dance/performance/sound, not visual-art-default) and a European/Mediterranean/East-Asian city-level focus.** Res Artis, TransArtists and ArtConnect are visual-art-shaped directories that dance/sound artists have to route around. Aerowaves/Life Long Burning/dancEUA are real and well-run but each is a single narrow mandate (one annual cohort, one project's partner calls, one nationality-focused program) — none is a continuously updated feed an artist checks the way Fellow.'s Hub is designed to work. On the Move is the closest in mission and tone (free, artist-first, mobility-focused, EU-weighted) but is a calls-and-guides *information network*, not a product with saved pipelines, deadline chips, or a personal feed.

2. **Verification and trust are not a differentiator anyone else is visibly making explicit.** None of the sources reviewed advertise a "verified by a person, re-checked on a date" trust stamp as a core feature the way HANDOFF_V3's trust-stamp requirement (Task 08) does. Several (Res Artis, TransArtists) rely on the listing organization to self-post and self-maintain accuracy. This is a real, checkable gap — not an assumption — but it is worth flagging that it was assessed by absence (no evidence found of the feature) rather than confirmed absence; a competitor could have this without it surfacing in search.

3. **No one combines the opportunities feed with a "what to do while you're physically in this city" layer for artists.** Bandsintown/Songkick do this for touring *musicians already on a booked tour* and their fans — a fundamentally different use case (promotion of a scheduled show vs. an artist proactively discovering what else exists in a city they'll be in). Nothing found combines "artist profile already knows where/when I'll be" with "surface workshops, other artists' showings, retreats, festivals in that window." This is Trip Radar's white space and it appears genuinely open, not just under-marketed.

4. **The "career profile for creatives" category has a real, instructive failure (Polywork) and no successful practice-shaped alternative to LinkedIn for performing artists specifically.** Portfolio builders (Cargo, Format, Adobe Portfolio/Behance) solve *presentation* but not *discovery or connection*. Contra solves client-marketplace matching for design/dev freelancers, not grant/residency/company-shaped careers. Are.na is a curation tool, not a profile. The gap is not "nobody has tried a LinkedIn-for-creatives" (Polywork tried, broadly) — it's that nobody has tried it **bundled with a reason to open the app weekly** (an opportunities feed, a trip radar) rather than as a standalone identity product. That bundling is Fellow.'s structural advantage over a Polywork-shaped competitor, and it is a testable hypothesis, not a proven one.

5. **Dance/performance/experimental-sound as a first-class discipline taxonomy, not an afterthought category, is unclaimed territory.** Every general aggregator checked (Res Artis, TransArtists, ArtConnect, CaFÉ, e-flux, ArtsHub) defaults to a visual-arts mental model (exhibitions, galleries, juried shows) with dance/performance/sound bolted on as one tag among many. The dance-specific sources that do exist (Aerowaves, Life Long Burning, dancEUA) are valuable but structurally can't scale into a general feed — they're each tied to one program's funding cycle and partner list.

**Net:** the white space is not "an opportunities aggregator" (several exist) or "a creative profile" (several have been tried, one prominently failed) — it's the specific combination of (a) performing-arts-native taxonomy, (b) verified/trustworthy curation as an explicit product feature, (c) a proactive location-aware layer no aggregator or touring-discovery tool currently offers, and (d) bundling profile + discovery so the identity layer has a reason to be visited regularly. No single fact found in this research contradicts that; several (Polywork's shutdown, the fragmentation of dance-specific calls, the absence of a Trip-Radar-shaped product) actively support it.
